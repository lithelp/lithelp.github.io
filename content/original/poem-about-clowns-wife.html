Clown's Wife byJohnson Agard

The Clown’s Wife

About my husband, the clown,

what could I say?

On stage, he’s a different person.

Up there he’s a king on a throne,

but at home you should hear him moan.

The moment he walks through that door

without that red nose and them funny clothes,

he seems to have the world on his shoulder.

I do me best to cheer him up, poor soul.

I juggle with eggs, I turn cartwheels,

I tell jokes, I do me latest card trick,

I even have a borrow of his red nose.

But he doesn’t say exactly how he feels,

doesn’t say what’s bothering him inside.

Just sits there saying almost to himself:

‘O life, ah life,

what would I do without this clown of a wife?’

Johnson Agard

The Clown’s Wife by John Agard

Introduction

“The Clown’s Wife” is a thought-provoking poem by the Guyanese-British poet John Agard. It explores the difference between a person’s public appearance and private reality through the life of a circus clown.

To the audience, the clown appears cheerful, energetic and amusing. At home, however, he is tired, unhappy and emotionally troubled. His wife tries to entertain him by imitating his circus performance, but she cannot remove his sadness. Through this reversal of roles, Agard shows that people who make others happy may experience deep suffering in their private lives.

Background

John Agard was born in Guyana in 1949 and later moved to Britain. His poetry frequently explores identity, culture, social expectations and human relationships. He is known for using simple language, humour, conversational rhythms and Caribbean speech patterns.

In this poem, Agard looks beyond the clown’s colourful appearance. He invites readers to consider the human being hidden beneath the costume and painted smile.

The situation

The speaker is the clown’s wife. She describes two different sides of her husband:

  • The public clown who entertains the circus audience

  • The private man who returns home unhappy and silent

At the circus, the clown performs confidently. He jokes, tumbles, sings and creates laughter. His profession requires him to appear cheerful regardless of his true feelings.

When he returns home, his behaviour changes completely. He sits silently and appears burdened by sadness. His wife tries to cheer him by copying the comic acts he performs at the circus. However, her efforts fail. Instead of laughing, he becomes even more distressed.

The speaker

The poem is narrated by the clown’s wife. This choice is important because she knows both his public and private personalities.

The circus audience sees only the performer. His wife sees the real person beneath the costume. Her account reveals the emotional cost of being expected to entertain others continuously.

The wife is presented as:

  • Loving

  • Concerned

  • Observant

  • Loyal

  • Patient

  • Sympathetic

  • Helpless before her husband’s sadness

She wants to make her husband happy, but she does not fully understand the cause of his suffering. Her inability to comfort him creates sadness and pathos.

The clown’s public personality

At the circus, the clown appears humorous, active and energetic. His movements, clothes and behaviour are designed to make the audience laugh.

He represents entertainment and happiness. The audience probably assumes that a person capable of producing so much laughter must also be happy.

However, his cheerfulness is part of a performance. It does not reflect his true emotional condition. His costume and behaviour create a public identity that conceals the real person.

The clown’s private personality

At home, the clown is very different. He is quiet, exhausted and sorrowful. The removal of his costume also removes the false appearance of happiness.

His sadness may result from:

  • The emotional burden of constantly pretending to be happy

  • Physical and mental exhaustion

  • Dissatisfaction with his profession

  • The contrast between his public role and private identity

  • Personal difficulties that are not explained in the poem

  • The feeling that people laugh at his foolish appearance without understanding him as a person

Agard does not explain the exact cause of the clown’s unhappiness. This makes the poem universal. The clown can represent anyone who hides personal pain behind a cheerful public appearance.

The circus performance

The wife first presents her husband in his professional environment. He performs before an audience and uses exaggerated movements to make people laugh.

The energetic description creates the familiar image of a clown. He appears to be completely connected with fun and amusement.

This opening prepares the reader for the strong contrast between his public behaviour and his private sadness.

The clown’s return home

A clear change occurs when the clown returns home. The lively public performer becomes a silent and unhappy man.

This change develops the theme of appearance versus reality. Home is the place where he no longer needs to perform. Therefore, his genuine emotions emerge.

The audience sees only his public mask, while his wife must face the sadness hidden beneath it.

The wife’s performance

When the wife sees her husband’s unhappiness, she attempts to entertain him. She imitates his circus behaviour and performs foolish actions.

This creates a reversal of roles:

  • At the circus, the husband entertains other people.

  • At home, the wife tries to entertain her husband.

  • The clown becomes the audience.

  • The wife becomes the performer.

The situation initially appears humorous. However, it gradually becomes sad because her efforts do not succeed.

The clown’s reaction

The clown does not respond to his wife’s performance with laughter. Instead, his emotional pain becomes more noticeable.

His reaction suggests that comic behaviour cannot solve genuine emotional suffering. The actions that entertain a circus audience may remind him of the burden of his profession.

The wife’s failure does not mean that she lacks love or concern. It shows that the clown’s unhappiness is too deep to be removed by a brief performance.

Appearance versus reality

The central theme of the poem is the difference between appearance and reality.

The clown appears happy before the audience, but he is deeply unhappy in private. His colourful costume and comic actions hide his real emotions.

The poem warns readers not to judge people merely by their outward appearance. A smile does not always indicate happiness, just as silence does not always indicate a lack of feeling.

Public identity and private suffering

The clown has two identities:

  • A public identity created for entertainment

  • A private identity known mainly to his wife

His profession forces him to hide his true emotions. This suggests that public expectations can become emotionally exhausting.

The clown must make other people laugh even when he does not feel cheerful. He is therefore separated from his genuine emotions while performing.

The loneliness of the entertainer

Although the clown is surrounded by a laughing audience, he remains emotionally isolated. The audience enjoys his performance but knows nothing about his private pain.

The poem suggests that popularity and public attention do not necessarily prevent loneliness. A person may be admired by many people and still feel unhappy and misunderstood.

Love and concern within marriage

The wife’s performance reveals her love for her husband. She is willing to make herself appear foolish in order to comfort him.

Her actions show:

  • Concern for her husband

  • Loyalty to him

  • A willingness to support him

  • An understanding that he needs emotional comfort

  • Her desire to restore happiness to their home

However, love does not automatically give her the power to solve his problems. The poem presents both the strength and the limitations of human love.

Failure of communication

The clown does not openly explain his feelings. His wife responds through actions rather than through a meaningful conversation.

Their relationship contains love, but it lacks effective communication. As a result, the wife can observe his sadness without understanding how to remove it.

The poem suggests that genuine communication is necessary when dealing with emotional pain.

Emotional labour

The clown’s profession requires him to create a particular emotion for other people. He must appear cheerful even when he does not feel happy.

This is a form of emotional labour. He must control or conceal his true feelings in order to perform his job successfully.

The poem recognizes the hidden emotional burden experienced by entertainers and by others whose work requires them to produce happiness, comfort or amusement.

Humour and sadness

The poem combines comic actions with serious emotional suffering. The wife’s imitation of the clown may appear amusing, but its purpose and failure make it painful.

This combination of humour and sadness reflects the traditional figure of the clown. A clown’s painted smile may hide personal sorrow.

The poem therefore contains both humour and pathos.

First-person narration

The wife narrates the poem in the first person. This makes the account personal and intimate.

Readers learn about the clown’s private life from someone who loves him. The wife’s narration also reveals her own concern, helplessness and emotional pain.

Dramatic monologue

The poem functions as a dramatic monologue because the wife describes her husband, her actions and her feelings in her own voice.

The husband does not speak directly. Readers must understand his emotions through his behaviour and through his wife’s observations.

This silence makes the clown appear even more emotionally isolated.

Contrast

Contrast is the most important technique in the poem.

At the circus, the clown is cheerful, active and entertaining. At home, he is quiet, tired and unhappy.

At the circus, he makes the audience laugh. At home, his wife cannot make him laugh.

His public appearance suggests happiness, while his private behaviour reveals suffering.

This contrast develops the central theme of appearance versus reality.

Role reversal

The wife takes over the clown’s role at home. The professional entertainer becomes the silent observer, while his wife becomes the performer.

This reversal creates humour because the wife attempts actions normally associated with her husband. However, it also creates sadness because her performance fails.

The role reversal allows the wife to experience the difficulty of trying to create happiness for someone else.

Irony

There is situational irony because the man who makes large audiences laugh cannot be made happy by similar comic actions.

It is also ironic that the wife’s attempt to create happiness appears to deepen his sadness.

The clown’s profession is associated with joy, yet his personal life is marked by unhappiness. This contradiction strengthens the poem’s message.

Symbolism

The clown’s costume and painted appearance symbolize a public mask. They hide his real emotions and allow him to present an artificial personality.

The costume may therefore symbolize:

  • False appearance

  • Social expectations

  • Professional identity

  • Hidden suffering

  • The separation between the public and private self

Removing the costume represents the clown’s return to his genuine identity.

Visual imagery

The descriptions of the clown’s movements, costume and performance create vivid pictures in the reader’s mind.

The wife’s imitation also produces comic visual images. These entertaining pictures are contrasted with the quiet sadness of the home.

The visual imagery makes the role reversal easy to imagine.

Colloquial language

Agard uses simple, informal and conversational language. The wife sounds like an ordinary person describing a personal experience.

This conversational style makes the poem accessible and emotionally convincing. It also helps readers feel close to the speaker.

Humour

Humour is created through exaggerated movements and the wife’s imitation of circus actions.

However, the humour has a serious purpose. It exposes the difference between temporary entertainment and genuine happiness.

The reader may smile at the wife’s actions but soon realizes that the situation is emotionally painful.

Repetition and listing

The listing of different comic actions creates rhythm and suggests the energy of a circus performance.

It also shows how hard the wife tries to make her husband respond. Each additional action increases the reader’s hope that the clown might laugh, making the failure more painful.

Tone and mood

The tone changes as the poem develops.

At the beginning, the tone is:

  • Light

  • Playful

  • Energetic

  • Humorous

Later, it becomes:

  • Concerned

  • Sympathetic

  • Sad

  • Helpless

  • Reflective

The overall mood is bittersweet because humour and sorrow exist together.

Significance of the title

The title is “The Clown’s Wife,” not simply “The Clown.” This directs attention towards the wife’s perspective and experience.

She is invisible to the circus audience, yet she carries the emotional burden of her husband’s private suffering. The title recognizes the person who supports the entertainer after the public performance ends.

It also shows that the poem is not only about a clown. It is about marriage, emotional support and the pain of watching someone one loves suffer.

Importance of the ending

The ending is effective because the wife’s comic performance fails to produce laughter. Instead, the clown’s sadness becomes more intense.

This result changes the meaning of all the humorous actions that came before it. Readers realize that the clown’s emotional problem cannot be solved through physical comedy.

The ending creates pathos and encourages readers to sympathize with both the husband and wife.

Central message

The poem teaches that outward appearances may conceal private suffering. A person who seems cheerful in public may be experiencing loneliness, exhaustion or emotional pain.

It also shows that genuine comfort requires more than temporary entertainment. People need understanding, communication and emotional support.

The poem encourages readers to look beyond social roles and recognize the human beings hidden behind them.

Critical appreciation

“The Clown’s Wife” is an effective exploration of the difference between public appearance and private reality. Through the wife’s simple and sympathetic narration, John Agard reveals that the cheerful clown seen by the audience is very different from the unhappy man who returns home.

The poem’s strength lies in its combination of humour and pathos. The wife’s imitation of her husband creates a comic role reversal, but her failure to make him laugh turns the situation into one of sadness. Contrast, irony, symbolism, visual imagery and conversational language help Agard expose the emotional suffering hidden beneath the clown’s public mask.

The poem has a universal message. Many people perform social or professional roles that do not reflect their true feelings. By presenting the clown through the eyes of his loving wife, Agard asks readers to respond to hidden suffering with understanding rather than superficial judgement.
